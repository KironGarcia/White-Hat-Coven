===== Block B — chatAula shape (always) =====

Voice: Gray Mage talking to Cláudia. Short. No CVE, no flag, no “user was stupid”. pt / en / es, same skeleton.

Three balloons (one sentence each; packed together as in Checkpoint 23):

1. '''What it is''' — costume / fantasy metaphor + the attack in one line.
2. '''Why people fall''' — fear, hurry, or trust. Always “ordinary people”.
3. '''What to do''' — practical rule + “if it already happened”.

Then one situation question (“what do you do?”) and three options:

* A — impulsive wrong (click fast / send the code).
* B — the right move (always index '''1''', the middle).
* C — silly wrong (forward it, post it in the group).

===== Block C — Reference lines (pt, do not invent a new tone) =====

E-mail:
1. Phishing by e-mail is a disguise: it looks like the bank or Netflix, but the address is fake.
2. They use ordinary people’s fear — “your account will close” — so someone clicks and hands over the password.
3. Before clicking, check the address. Never type a password on an unexpected link. If you already did, change it on the official site.

WhatsApp:
1. The scammer wears the name of someone you love and asks for Pix or an “urgent” code.
2. Ordinary people fall because they trust family. Hurry and emotion stop them from calling to check.
3. Never send the SMS code. Confirm with a call to a number you already know. Urgent Pix? Be suspicious.
